# The AI Operator Canvas

## Five Decisions Before Your Next Business AI Test

**Free field note from Mara Quinn, The Practical AI Operator**

A business does not have an AI strategy simply because people are trying AI tools. A useful test begins when the business makes clear decisions about the result, responsibility, information, review, and evidence.

Use this one-page canvas before your next AI experiment. Complete it with the person who owns the workflow—not only the person who found the tool.

---

## Decision 1 — Outcome

Do not begin with “use AI.” Name the business result you want to improve.

> We are testing AI in the ____________________ workflow to improve ____________________ without increasing ____________________.

Good outcomes are observable. Examples include reducing response delay, creating more consistent first drafts, shortening preparation time, or lowering avoidable rework. Avoid vague goals such as “become more innovative.”

## Decision 4 — Review

Decide where human judgment remains mandatory.

> AI may ____________________, but a person must review ____________________ before ____________________.

Use stricter review when the output affects money, legal rights, health, safety, employment, sensitive personal information, or an important customer relationship. A draft can be assisted. Responsibility cannot be delegated to a tool.

---

## The Test Brief

Complete the statement below in one paragraph:

> For the next ______ days, [OWNER] will test [AI-ASSISTED STEP] on [NUMBER OR TYPE OF ITEMS]. The AI may use [ALLOWED INPUTS] and may produce [OUTPUT]. A human will review the result before [CONSEQUENCE OR NEXT STEP]. We will continue, revise, or stop the test based on [MEASURE AND THRESHOLD].

If the team cannot explain the test this clearly, the scope is probably still too large.

---

## Review Questions

After the test, discuss what actually happened. Did the output improve the chosen result? Where did a person still need to intervene? Which errors repeated? Did the workflow create new work somewhere else? Is the next decision to expand, revise, pause, or stop?

Record the answer in plain language. A stopped test can still be valuable if it prevents a larger mistake.
