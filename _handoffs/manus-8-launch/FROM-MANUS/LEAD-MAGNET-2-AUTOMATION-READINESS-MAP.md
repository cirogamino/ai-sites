# The Automation Readiness Map

## Choose One Workflow Worth Simplifying

**Free field note from Mara Quinn, The Practical AI Operator**

Automation should not begin with a tool. It should begin with a repeated task that you understand well enough to describe.

Use this map to decide whether one workflow is ready for a small AI-assisted test. It will help you separate a promising first project from a process that is still too unclear, risky, or inconsistent to automate responsibly.

---

## Step 1 — Name the Repeated Work

Complete this sentence:

> Every [day / week / month / customer event], I or my team must ____________________, which usually requires ____________________.

A useful first workflow is narrow. “Run marketing” is not a workflow. “Turn the approved weekly article into three draft social posts” is narrow enough to map and test.

**Workflow name:** ________________________________________________

**Current owner:** _________________________________________________

**How often it happens:** __________________________________________

## Step 3 — Score Readiness

Give the workflow one point for every statement that is true.

| Readiness statement | Yes / No |
|---|---|

### Readiness Result

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0–3 | The process needs clarification before automation. Document how the work is actually performed. |
| 4–6 | The workflow may be ready for a narrow assisted test with human review. |
| 7–8 | This is a strong candidate for a small, measured pilot. Keep approval controls in place. |

## Step 4 — Place the Human Review Point

AI assistance should not remove responsibility. Decide where a person will inspect, approve, correct, or stop the result.

> The workflow must pause for human review after ____________________ and before ____________________.

The higher the consequence of an error, the earlier and more carefully that review should happen.

---
