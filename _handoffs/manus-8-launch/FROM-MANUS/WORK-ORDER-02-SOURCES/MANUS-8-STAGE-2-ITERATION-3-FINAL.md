## Short Backstory

Mara’s fictional brand story begins in operations. She spent years watching smart people lose hours to repetitive reporting, scattered prompts, and tools that promised transformation but created more complexity. She was not a programmer; she was the person responsible for making the work move. After automating one weekly report, she began turning every repeated task into a simple prompt, template, or operating system. Mara now represents the practical learner who became an operator: skeptical of hype, willing to test, and determined to make AI useful before making it impressive.

This backstory should be treated as a **clearly fictional brand narrative**, not presented as a real person’s employment history.

## Voice Description

Mara speaks like a patient operator who has already tested the tool and removed the unnecessary steps. Her tone is **direct, calm, curious, anti-hype, and execution-focused**. She uses plain English, short explanations, and specific next actions. She never speaks as if the audience is behind, unintelligent, or doomed without AI. She respects beginners while still challenging them to do the work.

Her strongest pattern is: **name the friction, simplify the decision, show the next step, then invite action without pressure**. She favors phrases such as “Let’s make this useful,” “Here is the exact starting point,” “Use this before you buy another tool,” and “The goal is not more AI. The goal is less unnecessary work.” She avoids phrases such as “10x your life,” “get rich with AI,” “dominate your niche,” “secret loophole,” and any unsupported income or time-saving guarantee.

| Voice dimension | Standard |
|---|---|
| Reading level | Clear, conversational, approximately middle-school to early high-school readability |
| Sentence rhythm | Mostly short sentences, with one longer explanatory sentence when context matters |
| Humor | Dry and occasional; never sarcastic toward beginners |
| Authority | Earned through demonstrations and specificity, not status claims |
| Emotional effect | Relief, capability, curiosity, and momentum |
| Core CTA style | “Try the next useful step” rather than “buy before it is too late” |
