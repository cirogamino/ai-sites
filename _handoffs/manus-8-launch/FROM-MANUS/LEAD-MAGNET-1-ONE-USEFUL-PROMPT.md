# One Useful Prompt

## A Five-Minute AI Practice Card

**Free field note from Mara Quinn, The Practical AI Operator**

You do not need a library of 500 prompts to become useful with AI. You need one real task, one clear instruction, and a short loop that helps you notice what changed.

This field note gives you that loop. Use it on work you already planned to do today. The goal is not to produce a perfect answer on the first attempt. The goal is to learn how a small change in your instruction affects the result.

---

## The Four-Part Prompt

Copy the structure below and replace the bracketed sections with your own details.

> **Goal:** Help me [describe the real result you need].  
> **Context:** This is for [audience, situation, project, or business]. The relevant background is [include only the facts the AI needs].  
> **Constraints:** Keep the result [length, tone, reading level, limits, exclusions, or required facts]. Do not [name anything that would make the answer unusable].  
> **Output:** Return the answer as [email, checklist, table, outline, script, plan, or another exact format].

### Example

> **Goal:** Help me draft a follow-up email after a discovery call.  
> **Context:** This is for a local-service business owner who wants a simpler lead-response process. We discussed missed calls, slow follow-up, and a two-week test.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the email under 180 words. Use plain English and a calm, professional tone. Do not use urgency, inflated claims, or jargon.  
> **Output:** Return a subject line, the email body, and one clear next-step question.

---

## The Five-Minute Loop

| Minute | Action | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose one task you already need to finish. | Is the desired result concrete? |
| 2 | Fill in the four-part prompt. | Did you give the AI the facts it actually needs? |
| 3 | Run the prompt once. | Which part is useful, vague, missing, or excessive? |
| 4 | Change one variable only. | Try a tighter constraint, clearer audience, or different output format. |
| 5 | Compare both results and keep the better instruction. | What did your single change improve? |

Do not change five instructions at once. If everything changes, you will not know which decision improved the answer.

---

## Three Fast Repairs

### If the answer is generic

Add one piece of context that only someone inside the situation would know. Name the audience, their current problem, what has already happened, and what the answer must help them do next.

### If the answer is too long

Set an exact limit and output structure. “Be concise” is subjective. “Use five bullets, each under 18 words” gives the tool a visible boundary.

### If the answer sounds unnatural

Describe the voice with observable rules rather than adjectives alone. Instead of “make it friendly,” try: “Use contractions, short sentences, plain English, and no sales clichés.”
