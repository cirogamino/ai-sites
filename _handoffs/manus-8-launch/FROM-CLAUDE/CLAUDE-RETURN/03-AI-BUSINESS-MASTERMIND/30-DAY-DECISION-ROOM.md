# The AI Business Mastermind -8 — 30-Day Decision Room
**Mara Quinn, The Practical AI Operator**

A self-guided framework. Four weekly decision cycles, each built around one real, current decision from your business, plus a final review. Work alone, on your own schedule, using the `OPERATOR-SCORECARD.csv` to log each step.

---

## Week 1 — Frame the Decision

**Purpose:** Most bad business decisions were badly framed before they were badly made.

**Step 1 — Name it precisely.** Write the decision as a single sentence with a real deadline: "By [date], I will decide whether to [specific choice]." Vague framing ("figure out marketing") doesn't count — narrow it to an actual choice.

**Step 2 — Name what's actually being decided.** Separate the decision from the anxiety around it. What specifically changes based on which way you go?

**Step 3 — Set your decision deadline.** If there's no real deadline, this isn't ready for the room yet — bring one that has one.

**Log in scorecard:** Decision statement, deadline, why it matters now.

---

## Week 2 — Pressure-Test the Options

**Purpose:** Find the option you're not seriously considering.

**Step 1 — List every real option**, including "do nothing" and "delay six months." Don't skip the boring ones.

**Step 2 — For each option, write the two most likely ways it goes wrong**, and what you'd see early if it were happening. (Use AI here if useful: "What are the two most likely ways this goes wrong, and what's the early warning sign?")

**Step 3 — Identify your actual constraint.** Money, time, energy, skill, or risk tolerance — which one is really limiting your choice? Most people think it's money when it's actually time or nerve.

**Log in scorecard:** Options considered, top risk per option, the real constraint.

---

## Week 3 — Make the Call

**Purpose:** A decision not written down isn't a decision — it's a lean.

**Step 1 — Choose.** Write which option you're taking and the single biggest reason.

**Step 2 — Write your reversal trigger.** "I will reverse this decision if [specific, observable thing] happens by [date]." If you can't write one, you haven't thought it through enough to act yet.

**Step 3 — Write who else needs to know**, and what you'll tell them, in one sentence each.

**Log in scorecard:** Final decision, reversal trigger, who's informed.

---

## Week 4 — Act and Watch

**Purpose:** The decision only counts once it touches the real world.

**Step 1 — Take the first real action** on the decision this week. Not planning — action.

**Step 2 — Check your reversal trigger** against what actually happened. Has it fired? Not yet? Note where things stand honestly.

**Step 3 — Write one thing you'd do differently** if you were framing this decision again from scratch.

**Log in scorecard:** Action taken, trigger status, what you'd change.

---

## Week 5 — Final Review

**Purpose:** One decision teaches you something. Four decisions in a row teach you your pattern.

Look back across all four weeks in your scorecard and answer, in writing:

1. Which decision am I most confident was right, and why?
2. Which decision am I least sure about, and what would tell me either way?
3. Across all four, is there a constraint that shows up every time? (Time, money, nerve, information, something else?)
4. What's one change to how I decide — not what I decide — that I'm taking into next month?

**Log in scorecard:** Your answers to all four, dated.

---

## The Operator's Rule
> A decision without a written reversal trigger isn't a decision — it's a hope.
